Sources & limits

Keep the source and the exercise separate.

  1. Muhammad Iqbal, The Development of Metaphysics in Persia (1908), introduction pp. x–xi and chapter V, pp. 112–116. Iqbal describes the book as a historical review. The reading prompt asks whose position a passage presents; it does not treat every reported doctrine as his own, or as his later account of khudī.
  2. Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, “Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases” (1974), Science 185(4157), pp. 1124–1131. Their account of anchoring concerns judgments influenced by starting points. The fictional review case is an original teaching example, not a replication or validated test.

The first-answer → challenge → revision sequence is a constructed practice. Its connection to Iqbal, doctoral work and writing is an interpretive application. It does not establish that this sequence reduces bias.
