Mīzān.

خودی · the self that holds its shape

Khudī is not confidence. It is resistance to dissolution.

Iqbal spent thirty years arguing one thesis against two opponents at once: against the materialism that says the self is an illusion produced by matter, and against the mysticism that says the self is an illusion to be dissolved into God. His answer to both is that the self is the most real thing there is — not a substance you have, but an activity you sustain, which becomes more real the harder it is pressed and evaporates the moment it stops resisting.

Everything the instrument measures is downstream of that one claim. This page is the claim itself — the part that does not change when you change a rubric.

خودی کو کر بلند اتنا کہ ہر تقدیر سے پہلے
خدا بندے سے خود پوچھے، بتا تیری رضا کیا ہے
Khudī ko kar buland itnā ke har taqdīr se pahle / Khudā bande se khud pūchhe, batā terī razā kyā hai
"Raise the self so high that, before each decree, God Himself asks the servant: tell Me, what is it that you want."
Bāl-e-Jibrīl
The couplet is almost always misread. It is not a boast, and it is not a promise that a strong enough self gets what it asks for. Read it against Iqbal's account of taqdīr below: destiny is not a script written in advance, it is the reach of a thing's own possibilities. A self that has grown has literally enlarged the set of futures available to it — so being asked what you want is not a reward for piety. It is a description of what a developed ego is.

Definition

What Iqbal means, and four things he does not

In the third and fourth lectures of The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam, Iqbal builds the ego out of the one thing he thinks is given without inference: the felt unity of your own mental life. You do not infer that the thought you had a moment ago and the one you are having now belong to the same person. That unity is not a thing among things — it is a directive energy, an act rather than an object, and it is known from the inside in a way nothing else is. What follows, in his reading, is that the ego is not a passenger in a causal world; it is a genuine centre of initiative, and freedom is the degree to which its own activity, rather than the mechanism around it, determines what happens next.

Two consequences do the real work. First, the ego admits of degrees. It is not on or off — it is more or less integrated, more or less capable of originating action rather than reacting, and this is measurable in a person's life if not on an instrument. Second, it can decay. An ego that stops acting, that outsources its judgment, that seeks relief from the tension of being a self, does not stay level. It thins.

That is the whole reason a page like this can exist without being a category error. If khudī were a fixed possession there would be nothing to measure. Because it is an activity sustained against resistance, its inputs leave traces — and traces can be audited.

Provenance. The paragraphs above are a restatement of Iqbal's argument in the Reconstruction (1930), not a quotation from it. The vocabulary — directive energy, the ego as act, freedom as the removal of obstruction — is his; the compression and the application are not. Go to the lectures themselves before citing any of it in your own work.

It is not

EgoIn the English pejorative sense. Iqbal's khudī is compatible with — and in his account requires — humility before God. What it refuses is humility before people, fashions, and circumstances.
Self-esteemA feeling about yourself. Khudī is a capacity to act. The feeling can be high while the capacity rots; that combination has a name and it is not a good one.
Self-optimisationIqbal's self is built to be spent on something — a work, a people, a responsibility. A self perfected and never deployed has failed the definition, not met it.
IndividualismHe wrote a second, longer poem specifically to prevent that reading. See Bekhudī below — it is the correction this instrument most needs.

Mechanism

What raises the self, and what thins it

In Asrār-i-Khudī Iqbal is unusually concrete about this. The self is not raised by contemplation of the self; it is raised by specific activities, and it is lowered by specific ones. The diagram is the argument: the ladder does not hold you at a rung. Absent upward force you descend, because a self that is not being strengthened is being spent.

Upward · what builds it

ʿIshq
عشق · love
 The chief force, and the one most often sentimentalised. In Iqbal it is directed intensity — the capacity to want one thing enough to organise a life around it. Not the feeling; the direction. It is why the instrument scores iḥsān and ẓabt together under this heading: love is what restraint is for, and restraint is what love costs.
Faqr
فقر · detachment
 Not poverty and emphatically not begging — indifference to what the world can withhold from you. A self whose conduct is contingent on approval, comfort or a salary has already handed someone else the controls. Faqr is what makes the other virtues affordable.
Himmat
ہمت · resolve
 Ambition in the older, harder sense: the willingness to attempt what you might fail at. Iqbal treats the safe life as a form of decay, because an ego only discovers its limits by pressing them.
Kasb-e-ḥalāl
کسبِ حلال · earned bread
 Living on what you have honestly produced. He makes this a condition of selfhood rather than a rule of commerce — the falcon in his imagery does not scavenge, and neither does a self that intends to remain one.
Taskhīr
تسخیر · assimilation
 Taking the world in and making something of it — knowledge, craft, mastery of an environment rather than accommodation to it. This is the strand of Iqbal that reads scientific and creative work as spiritual activity, and it is the one that most directly licenses the doctorate.

Downward · what thins it

Su'āl
سوال · asking
 Iqbal's harshest theme. Anything received rather than earned weakens the receiver — not because the giver is wrong, but because the self that habituates to being supplied stops generating. Read it broadly: the borrowed opinion, the unearned position, the answer taken from an assistant without being checked.
Taqlīd
تقلید · imitation
 Servile copying of a form whose reason you have not reconstructed. Not the same as learning from tradition — the test is whether you could rebuild it. A discipline you follow without knowing why is not making you; it is wearing you.
Yās
یاس · despair
 He classes hopelessness as an active destroyer, not an absence. The self that concludes in advance that the effort is futile has stopped being a cause. The Qur'an's prohibition on despair (39:53) reads, in his hands, as a structural requirement rather than a comfort.
Ghulāmī
غلامی · subjection
 Political in his writing, but the mechanism is general: any arrangement in which your action is determined elsewhere. Including the ones you have agreed to and the ones that pay well.
The trance
· self-dissolution
 The one that concerns this instrument most directly. See below — it is not a footnote in Iqbal, it is the fight he picked.
Iqbal, intoxication, and the fight he actually picked. The first edition of Asrār-i-Khudī (1915) contained an attack on Ḥāfiẓ so pointed that it provoked a public controversy in Indian literary circles, and Iqbal modified the passage in later editions. The target was never the man; it was an aesthetic — the whole Persianate register in which wine, the tavern and self-annihilation stand for the highest spiritual state. Iqbal's objection is structural rather than moral: a tradition that makes the dissolution of the self its summit will produce people who are magnificent at dissolving and useless at building, and he thought he could see exactly that around him.
Take that seriously and it lands somewhere uncomfortable and specific. The evening that softens the edges of the self is not a small private indulgence in Iqbal's scheme — it is the exact operation he identified as the mechanism of civilisational decay, performed on a Tuesday. That is a harder argument than any health claim, and it is the one worth sitting with. It is also, in fairness, his argument and not a ruling; he was a poet-philosopher making a polemical case, and he modified it under criticism. But if you are going to build a daily practice on his concept, this is the part of it that has your name on it. The ledger is next door.

Destiny

Taqdīr is not a script

The objection Iqbal has to answer is obvious: if everything is decreed, what is a strengthened self for? His move is to deny the premise that taqdīr means a fixed future written down in advance. Destiny, in his account, is the inward reach of a thing's own possibilities — what it is capable of becoming, unfolding from within rather than imposed from outside. Time, for him, is not a line already drawn along which you are dragged; it is the medium in which possibilities are made actual, and an ego is one of the things that makes them actual.

So the couplet is not asking for a magic clause. A self that has grown has a genuinely larger space of possibility — more it could do, more it could refuse, more it could sustain. Being asked what you want is what having such a self consists of, not a prize awarded for having one.

This is also why fatalism reads, in Iqbal, as a symptom rather than a doctrine — the theology a thinned self reaches for to explain its own inertia. Determinism is what a weak ego calls its situation.

Provenance. Paraphrase of Iqbal's treatment of time, destiny and the ego in the Reconstruction, particularly the lectures on the conception of God and on the human ego. Not quotation.

Time, and why a daily instrument is coherent

Iqbal distinguishes two ways of having time. Serial time is the clock — discrete, countable, external, the time in which today is a unit that ends at midnight. Duration is time lived from the inside, where the past is not gone but carried, and the self is a continuity rather than a sequence of episodes.

The daily sheet lives in serial time. Khudī lives in duration. That gap is not a flaw in the design — it is the reason the design works. You cannot act on duration directly. You can only act on the day in front of you, and trust that a self is what accumulates. The measurement is the handle; the thing being built is not the score.

It is also why this instrument shows streaks but never lets them gate the ladder. A streak is a serial-time artefact: break it and the counter says zero, which is a claim about the calendar and not about you. The fourteen-day median is a duration measure — it asks what has been carried, and it survives a bad Thursday because a self does.

ستاروں سے آگے جہاں اور بھی ہیں
ابھی عشق کے امتحاں اور بھی ہیں
Sitāroñ se āge jahāñ aur bhī haiñ / Abhī ʿishq ke imtiḥāñ aur bhī haiñ
"Beyond the stars there are worlds still — there are trials of love yet to come."
Bāl-e-Jibrīl

The correction

Bekhudī — and this instrument's blind spot

Three years after Asrār-i-Khudī, Iqbal published Rumūz-i-Bekhudī — "the mysteries of selflessness" — and it is longer than the first. He wrote it because the first poem had been read exactly the way you would expect: as a licence for a strong individual. His correction is that the individual self reaches its completion inside a community, that the millat is the medium in which a self becomes durable rather than merely impressive, and that an ego which has organised the world around its own development has not ascended the ladder — it has fallen off it while facing upward.

Which puts an obvious and uncomfortable question to a page like this one. A very well-instrumented self is still a self-facing project. Every measure here except one asks what you did for your own development. That is a real structural bias and it should be named rather than decorated over.

Three things partly answer it, and it is worth being honest that they only partly do. Iḥsān is a scored measure, deliberately defined as a transaction with a date on it rather than a disposition — a specific act toward a specific person. Phase four requires output: something must leave your hands each week, into the world, where other people are. And the whole apparatus is aimed at a doctorate whose subject is how professional judgment degrades — work whose entire value is to other people. The instrument is defensible as scaffolding for a life spent outward. It is indefensible as a life. If it ever becomes the interesting part, it has become the failure Iqbal wrote the second poem to prevent.

نہیں تیرا نشیمن قصرِ سلطانی کے گنبد پر
تو شاہیں ہے بسیرا کر پہاڑوں کی چٹانوں میں
Nahīñ terā nasheman qasr-e-sulṭānī ke gumbad par / Tū shāhīñ hai, baserā kar pahāṛoñ kī chaṭṭānoñ meñ
"Your nest is not on the dome of the royal palace. You are a falcon — make your dwelling in the rocks of the mountains."
Bāl-e-Jibrīl

The falcon, unpacked

Iqbal's shāhīn is his most compressed statement of the whole doctrine, and every element is load-bearing. It does not scavenge — kasb-e-ḥalāl. It does not nest in the palace — faqr; comfort is where selves go to thin. It keeps altitude — himmat and a long view. It is solitary but not idle. And it lives on the rock, which is to say: in conditions that keep it sharp, because for Iqbal an ego without resistance is an ego without definition.

What this instrument cannot do

It cannot measure khudī. It measures conditions under which khudī has historically been observed to grow, which is a weaker and more honest claim — the same distinction between a control's design and its outcome that you apply for a living. A person could score 90 for a year and be a thinner self at the end of it, if the scoring became the activity. That failure mode is real, it is not detectable from inside the data, and no refinement of the rubric will fix it.

The only external check is the one Iqbal himself would apply: what left your hands, and who is better off. That is why phase four exists and why it cannot be satisfied by a good week of scores.

Reading, in order

Asrār-i-Khudī 1915The doctrine of the self and its three stages. Nicholson's The Secrets of the Self is the standard English translation and carries a useful introduction.
Rumūz-i-Bekhudī 1918The communal correction. Read it second and read it as an argument with the first, because that is what it is.
The Reconstruction 1930The philosophical case in prose — the ego, time, freedom, destiny. The lectures on the human ego and on the conception of God carry most of what this page paraphrases.
Bāl-e-Jibrīl 1935The mature Urdu poetry, and the source of the couplets quoted here.

Maturity model

The Khudī ladder

In Asrār-i-Khudī (1915) Iqbal sets out three stages in the education of the self: obedience to the law, then self-control, then vicegerency — the self strong enough to be entrusted with the world. It maps almost exactly onto the Qur'an's three namings of the nafs. It also maps onto something you already know how to read: a control maturity rating, computed on evidence, revisable downward.

The Qur'an's three selves

إِنَّ ٱلنَّفْسَ لَأَمَّارَةٌۢ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ إِلَّا مَا رَحِمَ رَبِّىٓ
"Indeed the self is a persistent enjoiner of evil, except those on which my Lord has mercy."
Yūsuf 12:53 · an-nafs al-ammārah — the self that commands
وَلَآ أُقْسِمُ بِٱلنَّفْسِ ٱللَّوَّامَةِ
"And I swear by the self-reproaching soul."
Al-Qiyāmah 75:2 · an-nafs al-lawwāmah — the self that argues back
يَـٰٓأَيَّتُهَا ٱلنَّفْسُ ٱلْمُطْمَئِنَّةُ ٱرْجِعِىٓ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكِ رَاضِيَةً مَّرْضِيَّةً
"O soul at peace, return to your Lord, well-pleased and well-pleasing."
Al-Fajr 89:27–28 · an-nafs al-muṭma'innah — the settled self
Note the middle stage. The self-reproaching soul is sworn by — the internal argument is not a symptom of failure, it is the sound of the second stage working. Most people quit there because they mistake the noise for regression.

جہادِ زندگانی میں ہیں یہ مردوں کی شمشیریں
Yaqīn-e-muḥkam, ʿamal-e-paiham, moḥabbat fātiḥ-e-ʿālam / Jihād-e-zindagānī meñ haiñ ye mardoñ kī shamshīreñ
"Firm conviction, ceaseless action, love that conquers the world — in the struggle of life, these are a man's swords."
Bāl-e-Jibrīl · the three faculties this instrument scores
What Iqbal is not saying. Khudī is not ego in the English sense and not self-esteem. It is selfhood — a centre of will that becomes more real the more it acts, resists dissolution, and takes responsibility. In the Reconstruction lectures he argues the self is sharpened by contact with resistant reality, not by escaping it. Which is why his sharpest polemic is against inherited quietism: the trance, the withdrawal, the pleasant dissolving of the self into a mood. He would have had a specific and unkind word for anything that softens the edges of the self on a Tuesday night.

The reference line

What is being measured, and on whose authority

Seven measures, each anchored to a verse and each carrying a fixed weight. Scoring is 0–3 against a written rubric, because a scale without definitions is a mood ring. References are given by sūrah:āyah so you can verify every one against a muṣḥaf — renderings below are working translations, not a substitute for the Arabic.

وَٱلسَّمَآءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ ◆ أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِى ٱلْمِيزَانِ ◆ وَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلْوَزْنَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا۟ ٱلْمِيزَانَ
"And the heaven He raised, and He set the balance — that you not transgress within the balance. So establish weight in justice, and do not fall short in the balance."
Ar-Raḥmān 55:7–9 · the instrument is named in the Book before it is named here

Weighting

Weights are a judgment, and judgments should be declared rather than smuggled in. Ṣalāh carries the most because it is the only measure with a fixed external clock — everything else can be rationalised, that one cannot. Change them in Settings if you disagree; the change is logged against the day it takes effect.

Three faculties

Yaqīn · conviction · ṣalāh + dhikr
The measures with a fixed form. Iqbal's point is that form precedes feeling: the discipline installs the certainty, not the reverse.
ʿAmal · action · work + attention + body
Khudī is proved in output. "And that there is nothing for man except what he strives for" — 53:39.
ʿIshq · love · iḥsān + restraint
Restraint sits under love deliberately. In Iqbal, zabt is not repression; it is what love pays. Restraint without love produces a brittle self that snaps.

Arbaʿīn · forty days

The path, phased

Forty is the classical interval — the period of Mūsā's appointment (2:51, 7:142), the age of prophethood, the traditional length of a spiritual discipline. It is also, conveniently, long enough for a behaviour to stop requiring negotiation. Four phases of ten days. Each phase adds; nothing is dropped. The instrument only holds you to the measures the current phase has switched on — scoring all seven from day one is how this fails in week two.

Why this order and not another

Form before feeling. Phase one changes almost nothing except that it starts measuring. An auditor does not remediate during the walkthrough — you take the baseline first, or you will never know what moved. Ten days of honest logging with zero moralising is not a soft start, it is the control period, and skipping it is why previous attempts had nothing to point at.
Restraint second, not first. Phase two tightens once, on one thing: the intoxicant does not precede the day's ʿamal. Not "quit" — reorder. Everything you want from it is still available at the end of the day; what is removed is its function as an escape hatch, which is the only function that is actually costing you.
Love third, because restraint alone goes brittle. Iqbal is emphatic on this and it is the part every self-improvement scheme misses. A self built only from prohibition is thin and it snaps under load. Phase three adds the two measures that put something in — the night portion, and one concrete act toward another person daily. This is the phase that decides whether the other thirty days survive.
Output last, because that is the whole point. Niyābat is not a feeling of closeness. It is being trusted with something. Phase four requires you to ship — a chapter section, a lecture built, a tool published, a comment letter filed. Khudī is proved in creation. A self-audit that produces only a well-audited self has failed its own test.

"Indeed God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves."

When it breaks

لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا
"Do not despair of the mercy of God. Indeed God forgives all sins."
Az-Zumar 39:53
It will break. The failure mode is not the broken day — it is the four unlogged days after it, when the instrument goes dark precisely when it would have been most useful. Log the bad day. Score it honestly. Close it. A logged relapse is a data point in a system that is still running. An abandoned week is a system that stopped, and stopped systems are the only ones that never recover.

"God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity."
Al-Baqarah 2:286
