Case study · Measurement design

An instrument for what resists measurement

I spent twenty years auditing control environments, then built a measuring instrument and pointed it at myself. The interesting part was not what it measured. It was how much of ordinary self-assessment stops working the moment you refuse to let the subject award their own rating.

The problem the instrument was built for

My doctoral research asks a narrow question: when an AI system produces the analysis, does the reviewer still review it? The difficulty is not finding an answer. It is that the failure leaves no trace. Nobody documents the moment they stopped thinking. There is no workpaper entry for deference, no exception report for the reviewer who agreed because agreeing was easier.

Which means you cannot examine for it directly. You can only infer it from design — from whether the arrangement makes disagreement possible, cheap, and expected, or merely permitted in principle.

That is a general problem in governance and it is not confined to AI. Every control function eventually has to assess something that resists measurement: culture, challenge, independence, judgment. The usual response is a self-assessment questionnaire, which measures the one thing nobody needed measured — how the subject would like to be seen.

So I built an instrument on the opposite premise, and I built it on myself first, because that is the only subject I am entitled to experiment on.

Five decisions, and what each one costs

These are the transferable part. Each was a genuine trade — the instrument is worse in some respect for every one of them, and better in the respect that mattered.

Decision

The rating is computed, never awarded

The headline figure is derived from the measures underneath it. There is no field in which the subject records how they think they are doing, and no way to move the number except by moving an input that has its own written rubric.

What it costs: the instrument cannot capture anything the rubric did not anticipate. A genuinely unusual day scores as an ordinary one. That is a real loss and I accepted it, because a scoring field that can be reached directly will be reached directly, particularly on the days when the score would otherwise be bad.

What it asks of a governance function If your control self-assessment has a box where the owner states the residual rating, you are not measuring the control. You are measuring the owner's appetite for a difficult conversation.
Decision

The rating can be lost

Standing on the ladder is a computed position, and it is not a ratchet. Absent sustained input it falls. A measure you can only accumulate is not a measure of a capacity — it is a record of having once qualified.

What it costs: it is demoralising by construction, and it removes the single most reliable engagement mechanic available to anything that scores a person. The instrument is less pleasant to use than it could be. That was the point.

What it asks of a governance function Ask of any maturity rating, any certification, any "tier" in a vendor questionnaire: what would make this go down, and has it ever gone down for anyone? If there is no mechanism and no precedent, it is a badge.
Decision

Streaks are displayed but never allowed to decide anything

The instrument counts consecutive days, because a streak is genuinely motivating. It also refuses to let a streak gate the rating, and puts a rolling fourteen-day median in the load-bearing position instead.

Why: a streak that breaks reports zero. That is a true statement about the calendar and a false one about the person. The median asks a different question — what has actually been carried — and it survives one bad Thursday, because a capacity does.

What it asks of a governance function Most operational KPIs are streaks wearing other clothes: days since incident, consecutive clean months, zero-findings quarters. They are all fragile in the same way and they all create an incentive to protect the counter rather than the outcome it stands for.
Decision

Demonstration data is quarantined from every claim

The instrument can generate synthetic history, so that the charts are legible before real history exists. Every synthetic record is tagged, and anything tagged is excluded from the streak count and from the calibration statistics while remaining in the charts.

The rule underneath it: for each statistic, ask whether the number is a claim about the subject or a demonstration of the display. Claims exclude synthetic data. Displays may include it. Getting that split wrong is how a demo becomes a lie without anyone deciding to lie.

What it asks of a governance function The same question applies to every model dashboard built on backfilled, imputed or vendor-supplied data. Which figures on it are assertions, and does the interface distinguish them from the ones that are illustration?
Decision

Every claim carries its provenance, including the ones I wrote

Where the instrument states a classical position, it says whether that is a quotation or my restatement of an argument, and names the source. Passages compressing Iqbal's lectures are labelled as compressions, with the reader sent to the lectures before citing any of it.

What it costs: the prose is slower and less quotable. It also means the instrument cannot borrow authority it has not earned, which is the entire reason the labels are there.

What it asks of a governance function This is the discipline an examiner is applying when they ask where a number came from. It is worth applying to your own documentation before someone applies it for you — particularly now that a great deal of analysis arrives already drafted.

Why the frame is Iqbal, and what that does and does not license

The instrument scores a set of measures against a ladder drawn from Muhammad Iqbal's account of khudī — selfhood — as he develops it in Asrār-i-Khudī (1915) and the lectures published as The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam (1930).

The reason is structural rather than devotional. Iqbal's claim is that the self is not a possession but an activity sustained against resistance — that it admits of degrees, and that it thins when it stops acting, outsources its judgment, or seeks relief from the tension of being a self. A fixed possession could not be measured at all. An activity leaves traces, and traces can be audited.

He also names, with some precision, what thins it. One of those is su'āl — asking — read broadly as the habit of receiving rather than generating: the borrowed opinion, the unearned position, the answer taken and not checked. I did not go looking for a 1915 description of automation bias. It is simply the closest thing I have read to the mechanism my research is about.

What that licenses is a claim about a recurring structure, made in my own voice and carrying my own evidence. What it does not license is treating any of it as authority. Nothing on this page rests on a religious premise, and none of it is offered as one. If the argument above needs the citation to stand, the argument is bad.

“Beyond the stars there are worlds still — there are trials of love yet to come.”

Iqbal · Bāl-e-Jibrīl
Provenance. The paragraphs above restate Iqbal's argument; they do not quote it, apart from the couplet, which is given in translation. The vocabulary — the ego as act, freedom as the removal of obstruction, the self admitting of degrees — is his. The compression and the application to control design are mine, and the application is not a reading he would necessarily have endorsed.

Where the design is weak

Iqbal wrote the objection himself. Three years after the poem on selfhood he published a longer one, Rumūz-i-Bekhudī, precisely because the first had been read as a licence for a strong individual. His correction is that a self completes itself inside a community, and that an ego which has organised the world around its own development has not climbed the ladder — it has fallen off it while facing upward.

That objection lands on this instrument directly. A very well-instrumented self is still a self-facing project. Nearly every measure asks what the subject did for their own development, and no amount of careful scoring fixes a bias that sits in the choice of what to score.

Three things partly answer it and it is worth being honest that they only partly do. One measure is defined as a transaction with a date on it — a specific act toward a specific person, not a disposition. A later phase requires output: something must leave your hands and reach other people. And the whole apparatus is pointed at research whose value, if it has any, is to other people.

The instrument is defensible as scaffolding for a life spent outward. It is indefensible as a life. If it ever becomes the interesting part, it has become the failure the second poem was written to prevent.

Why you cannot see it

A reasonable question, given that everything else I build is public.

The instrument holds a real person's daily record, and that person is me. It runs entirely in the browser, makes no network request of any kind — prayer times are computed from solar geometry in JavaScript specifically so that nothing has to be fetched — and stores everything in local storage on one device. No reading has ever been committed to a repository, and none will be. What is publishable is the design, which is what this page is. What is not publishable is the data, and the distinction is not a matter of preference: an instrument that would let me show you its contents would be a different and much worse instrument.

The same principle governs the doctoral work. That study runs under IRB-25-0462, and no participant response appears in any repository I control, by design and by tested exclusion.
