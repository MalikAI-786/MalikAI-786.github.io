# Register — writing the tradition into a professional feed

He asked for one thing above all others: **keep LinkedIn fully professional.**
He also wants the feed built on his life theories, which are Qur'anic and
Iqbalian. Those two requirements are not in conflict, but holding them together
takes a rule, because the failure mode is quiet and only visible from outside.

## The rule

**The tradition is where the idea came from. It is never the authority for the
claim.**

Every post must work as a complete governance argument for a reader who skips
the Arabic, does not know who Iqbal was, and does not share the faith. The
citation earns its place by being *interesting* and *honest about provenance* —
never by being the reason the reader should believe the point.

The test, applied to every draft: **delete the citation. Does the argument
still stand?**

- If it stands, and the citation was adding depth, origin, or a better name for
  something the reader already recognised — keep it. That is the feed working.
- If it collapses, the post is asking a professional audience to accept a
  religious premise. Rebuild it. This is preaching, and it will read that way to
  exactly the audience he most wants.

## Why this is the commercially right call as well as the honest one

The audience is examiners, model risk leads, audit executives, compliance
officers and academics. A meaningful share of them are not Muslim, and
essentially all of them are at work when they read. A post that requires them
to accept scripture as evidence loses them on the first line, and — worse — it
recategorises him. He stops being the bank examiner with an unusual intellectual
lineage and becomes a religious poster who used to work in banking. That
reclassification is very hard to reverse and it closes the speaking and teaching
doors this feed exists to open.

The version that works is the opposite, and it is genuinely rare: a governance
practitioner who can show that a fourteen-hundred-year-old idea about
self-examination and a 1930 lecture on selfhood name a control problem the
profession is currently rediscovering badly. That is not a devotional post. It
is an intellectual-history post with an operational payload, and there is close
to nobody else on the platform who can write it from inside the audit seat.

## How the citations behave

Follow the provenance discipline already established in `mizan/khudi/`, which
distinguishes what Iqbal argued from what is a restatement of Iqbal.

- **Quote when quoting.** Give the source — `Bāl-e-Jibrīl`, `Asrār-i-Khudī`,
  *The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam* (1930), the sūra and verse.
- **Say when restating.** "This is a compression of his argument, not a
  quotation" is a sentence that belongs in a post as much as in the instrument.
- **Never improve a translation to fit a point.** If the standard rendering is
  awkward, use it awkwardly and note it.
- **Never attribute a modern governance concept to a classical source.** Iqbal
  did not write about model risk. The claim is that the structure recurs, and
  that claim is the author's, made in his own voice, with his own evidence.
- **One citation per post.** Two makes it a lecture.

## Transliteration and typography

Use the diacritics already used across the site and the instrument: khudī,
faqr, himmat, su'āl, muḥāsaba, iḥsān, ẓabt, taskhīr, Mīzān, kasb-e-ḥalāl. The
LinkedIn composer handles the characters. Gloss each term in plain English at
first use in a post — every post is somebody's first — and then use the term
normally, without re-glossing.

Arabic script is fine in the instrument, where there is space for it. In a post
it eats the fold budget and does not render reliably in every client. Use the
transliteration in the body; the script can appear in an image if one is used.

## What never appears

- Any claim about what God wants, requires, or rewards.
- Any implication that the reader should adopt the practice, the faith, or the
  instrument.
- Any framing that positions him as a teacher of religion. He is a bank
  examiner and a doctoral researcher who reads Iqbal seriously. That is the
  whole persona and it is more than enough.
- Personal devotional detail. What he prays, fasts, records or scores is his,
  and Safeguard 3 covers it. The instrument's *design* is publishable. His
  *readings* are not, ever, in any form, including as an anonymised example.
- Anything about anyone else's practice or observance.

## The line that keeps getting crossed elsewhere, for reference

The common failure on this platform is the post that opens with a religious or
philosophical quotation, spends three lines on it, and then attaches a business
lesson that the quotation does not actually support. It is recognisable
instantly and it is corrosive to credibility.

The structural difference here is that the argument runs the other way: he has
spent twenty years watching a specific thing go wrong in audit functions, and
he found that Iqbal had already described its mechanism. The post reports a
correspondence he noticed. It does not deduce a business lesson from scripture.
Keep that direction of travel and the register takes care of itself.
