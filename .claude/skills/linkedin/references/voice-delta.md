# Voice — what changes on LinkedIn, and what does not

`tools/newsletter/voice.md` governs. Read it first and treat it as binding.
This file records only the deviations the platform forces, and the reasoning
for each, so that nobody widens them later by accident.

## What does not change

Everything that matters. Short declarative sentences. Antithesis where the
distinction is the argument, and not twice in a paragraph. Open on the hook,
never on the author. Concrete before abstract. The reader is a peer. Plain
about uncertainty. No hype, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no "leverage" as a
verb, no corporate hedging, no summary paragraph that restates the post.

The four-part shape holds too: **hook, record, turn, open question.** It is the
same architecture as a newsletter issue, executed in a tenth of the words.

## The three deviations

### 1. Paragraphing

`voice.md` bans "LinkedIn cadence — no one-line-per-paragraph stacking for
rhythm". The ban stands, and it is about rhythm, not about line breaks.

Permitted: paragraphs of one to three sentences with a blank line between them.
A phone screen is roughly forty characters wide and a seven-line block of prose
on it does not get read.

Still banned: the stack. Six consecutive single-line paragraphs, each landing
like a revelation, is the house style of the feed this one is trying not to
be. The test is whether the breaks follow the argument's joints or manufacture
a rhythm the argument does not have.

Also still banned: "Here's the thing:", the rhetorical question used as a
section break, the one-word paragraph, and the ellipsis-as-suspense.

### 2. Length

A newsletter issue runs to a thousand words and can afford to develop. A post
gets 3,000 characters.

**Target 1,200 to 2,400 for a post carrying a developed argument.** Below about
900 the argument is usually asserted rather than made, which in this feed is
worse than being long. Above 2,400, check honestly whether a second argument has
crept in — that is what the upper bound is really policing.

Length is not the constraint. **One finding, developed once** is the constraint,
and it is the only one that matters. The instinct to add a second point because
there is room is the instinct that produces the two-argument post banned in
`SKILL.md`, and it will show up as length before it shows up as anything else.
When a draft runs long, do not trim evenly — find the second argument and cut
it out whole.

### 3. The close

A newsletter issue ends on the open question and stops. A post ends on the open
question and then, after a blank line, carries one line pointing at Proof Over
Promise, and then the hashtags.

That line is not a pitch. It says what the newsletter would give the reader who
wants the longer version, in his register, once. "The long version of this
argument runs in Proof Over Promise" is in voice. "Subscribe for more insights
like this!" is not, and would undo the post that preceded it.

## The register check, adapted

`voice.md` asks: does this sound like someone who has actually sat in the room,
or like someone describing the room?

On LinkedIn add a second: **would this survive being read aloud by someone who
disagrees with it?** The feed's audience includes examiners, model risk leads
and audit executives who have seen a great deal of confident nonsense about AI.
A sentence that only works on a reader already nodding is a sentence that will
be quoted back with a screenshot.

## One thing the platform rewards that the voice already does

Specificity. The posts that travel in this audience are the ones that name the
control, the document, the year and the thing that went wrong. That is already
rule 4 of `voice.md`. There is no tension to manage here — the honest version
and the effective version are the same version, which is the only reason this
feed is worth running at all.
