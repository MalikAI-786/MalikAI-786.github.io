## 2 · The set and how it is rehearsed

Texts, transliteration, stanza choices and sources: see the Iqbal folder in the session scratchpad (never committed; the texts are public domain, the stanza choices and notes are yours).

The rehearsal **is** the daily exercise you already have. The 07:00 articulation drill (breath first, jaw, final consonants, the pen, the pause) runs straight into the 07:15 rehearsal block. The drill's one rule carries over: **do not fill the pauses.** A couplet in a mehfil is two half-lines and a silence; the silence is where the "wah" comes.

Order on the night, and why:
1. **Lab pe aati hai dua** — everyone knows it, so the room settles and joins. Read slow; do not sing it unless the host asks for tarannum.
2. **Shikwa** — bands 1, 13, 14, 18, as one block. The daring one. Hold the mic still; let the lines do the work.
3. **Jawāb-e-Shikwa** — bands 1, 11, 13, 36, as one block (11 answers 13, 13 answers 14, 36 answers 18), ending on *Ki Muhammad se wafa tu ne to hum tere hain*. That is the line the room is waiting for; land it and stop. Optional if the slot is a full 8 minutes: Shikwa 31 + Jawāb 32 before the finale. Not recommended here: Shikwa 22 (*tu bhi to harjaai hai*) — it is the line that scandalised Lahore and it lands badly at a family dinner.

Texts are Rekhta's (Kulliyāt-e-Iqbāl, Delhi 2014) cross-checked against Urdu Wikisource and Pritchett; three Rekhta typos corrected, variants recorded. Shikwa's first recital is dated April 1909 by some sources and April 1911 by others — say "around 1911" and move on. Jawāb, 1913, is undisputed. Recite spoken (*tahtul-lafz*), not sung: Iqbal himself refused to recite Shikwa in tarannum.
4. **The original piece** (if the host gives the time) — see §5.

Open with the host's name and one sentence: who you are and why Iqbal. Thirty seconds, not three minutes.

## 3 · Dress

Formal mehfil register, no costume: a black or charcoal sherwani, or a black waistcoat over a white or off-white kurta-shalwar; dark shoes (not sneakers); a Karakul / Jinnah cap is correct for Iqbal Day and reads in photographs. One colour on the whole figure plus white. No logo, no watch that catches the light, nothing that moves when you breathe. Pocket square only if the sherwani has the pocket. The card goes in the inside pocket where you can reach it without looking.

## 5 · The tribute, without copying

Ayan Maqbool is a spoken-word performer: short lines, long silences, delivered low. The tribute is **to the delivery, not the words**. No line of his is quoted or paraphrased.

Method (this is the 3-6-9 practice put to work):
- Each morning this week, your 3-6-9 intention line is the seed. Write it 3×, then write the one sentence *underneath* it: what you are actually asking for.
- By Friday you have five sentences. Keep three. Those are the piece. Urdu where it comes in Urdu, English where it comes in English; do not translate yourself.
- Deliver it the way the drill taught you: breath, then line, then the full stop held. Sixty to ninety seconds. Introduce it in one sentence: "A short piece of my own; the way it is read, I learned from Ayan Maqbool."
- It is posted as a reel only if you are proud of it on Saturday. Otherwise it stays in the room.

## 6 · Making it travel

What travels is not the poem; it is **a man in a sherwani in a New Jersey restaurant reading Shikwa to a room that answers back.** Every clip needs the room in it.

Formats, in the order they go out:
1. Announcement post (Canva draft) → feed + story, tag the venue.
2. Poll story: "which stanza first?" → comments are the algorithm's fuel.
3. Dress-run teaser: 15 s, no poem audio, caption "Monday." (or Sunday).
4. Night-of stories: three raw clips, no editing, as they happen.
5. Three reels, one piece each, posted on consecutive days. Each follows the five-beat grammar in `.claude/skills/roohe-iqbal/references/content-system.md`: contradiction, the line, plain reading, the turn, the source.
6. The sources carousel: one slide per couplet, collection and year on every slide.
7. LinkedIn: 200 words, "why an auditor reads Iqbal out loud", one still, link to the page.

Rules from the Rooh-e-Iqbal skill still hold: collection on screen every time; nothing attributed without a source; saves and shares over likes. Ask the venue to repost; ask three friends in the room to post and tag `#BazmeIqbal2026 @rooheiqbal`.

## 7 · Running this with ChatGPT and Grok

Neither can post to Instagram for you, and nothing here should be posted by a machine anyway. They do three jobs well:

**ChatGPT (voice mode) — the rehearsal coach.** Paste once:
> You are my recitation coach for an Urdu poetry evening on Iqbal Day. I will read a piece aloud. After each reading, tell me only three things: where I ran out of breath, which full stops I did not hold, and one line to read again. Do not praise. Do not change the text. The pieces are Lab pe aati hai dua, selected stanzas of Shikwa and Jawab-e-Shikwa, and a short original piece.

**ChatGPT — captions and the LinkedIn post.** Paste the caption structure from the content system (argument first, couplet, transliteration, plain English, collection, one question, restrained hashtags) with the stanza you used, and ask for three variants. You pick one; you post it.

**Grok — distribution and timing.** Ask it, the morning after:
> What is being said on X about Iqbal Day 2026 this week, and which three accounts are driving it? Give me the handles and the posts, not a summary.
Then post your clip quoting one of those threads. Grok reads the room; you still write the line.

**Claude (this repo)** — sources and provenance, the page, the card, the calendar, and the weekly read of what actually moved.

The sequence is the same each time: Claude checks the source → you rehearse with ChatGPT → you perform → you post → Grok tells you where the conversation is → you reply there. No step is skipped and no step is automated past "draft".
