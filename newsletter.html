Editorial threads

What I write about

Five threads I keep returning to, drawn from my doctoral research and from work I have actually shipped inside regulated institutions.

Thread 01 · Governance

Frameworks, and what they quietly leave to you

NIST AI RMF, SR 26-2, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act. I read these for a living. What each one actually requires, where they overlap, and the judgment calls they hand back to you without admitting it.

Thread 02 · Judgment

What happens when the analysis arrives already formed

My doctoral work at FIU, in plain language. Automation bias, over-reliance, and the uncomfortable part: the machine is most agreeable exactly where a reviewer most needs to be contradicted.

Thread 03 · Evidence

The documentation I would ask you for

Written from the examiner's seat, because that is the seat I sit in. What I ask for, in what order, and why answering "we have a policy" ends the conversation badly.

Thread 04 · Ethics

Teaching it, rather than policing it

The auditor everyone dreads teaches nobody. You cannot examine your way out of deference, because nobody documents the moment they stopped thinking. So the question is how you teach judgment instead, using the same discipline.

Thread 05 · Practice

Things I built, including what broke

What it took to put AI into a live audit function, including the parts of mine that did not work. A record that only contains successes is not a record.

  • Internal audit, risk and compliance leaders being asked to assure AI they did not build
  • Model risk teams extending SR 26-2 practice to systems that do not behave like models
  • Anyone preparing to explain their AI controls to a regulator
  • Researchers working near judgment, automation bias and professional skepticism
How I write it. I use AI tools heavily, which is precisely why I study how they fail. Drafting assistance is used and disclosed. The writer

Who I am

Yasir A. Malik. I examine banks for a living, as a state examiner at the Florida Office of Financial Regulation. Before the regulator I spent fifteen years in internal audit at Citigroup and JPMorgan Chase, finishing as a Vice President. I have also built with these tools: I put a retrieval assistant into a live audit function and took about a third off the cycle time, which is exactly why I do not trust the demo.

I am a doctoral candidate at Florida International University, expected 2028. My research asks a narrow question with wide consequences: what happens to a professional's judgment when the answer arrives already formed, and confident, and usually right.

I grew up in Lahore and went to Aitchison College, whose motto I have never managed to put down: Perseverance Commands Success. This is where I think out loud along the way.
