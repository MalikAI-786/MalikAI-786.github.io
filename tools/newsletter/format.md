# The format

`voice.md` says how it sounds. This says how it is *built* — the container that
repeats every issue so readers learn the shape and subscribing feels like
joining something with a rhythm.

Derived from the McKinsey **CEO Shortlist** (issue read 2026-08-21, "Power and
growth in the AI age"), which is the model Yasir reads and wants to work in.
What follows is the structure stripped of McKinsey's chrome and re-fitted to a
single-author newsletter written from the examiner's seat.

---

## What McKinsey actually does

Worth naming precisely, because the useful parts are not the obvious ones.

| Move | How they do it | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Standing masthead | *The CEO Shortlist · Emerging ideas for leaders* | Same two lines every issue. The reader knows what they opened. |
| Cadence stated **inside** the issue | "a biweekly newsletter of our best ideas for the C-suite" | The promise is renewed every time, not buried on a signup page. |
| Named humans, reachable | "Curated by Alex Panas and Becca Coggins", both direct emails, signed "—Alex and Becca" | A masthead with a face and an inbox is a different object from a brand broadcast. |
| One-sentence thesis up front | "two arenas where hesitation carries a high cost" | Tells you the through-line before the items. |
| **Question as the header** | "The need for data capacity?" · "Can AI accelerate growth?" | Every item is framed as a question the reader also has. |
| The counterintuitive turn | "The greater near-term risk may be the reverse: too little capacity." | Each item earns its place by inverting the expected conclusion. |
| One hard number per item | $500M at Reckitt · $5.1B into humanoids · 2% of value by 2045 | Specific, checkable, memorable. Never a vague "significant." |
| Two items. Not five. | The whole issue is two ideas | Short enough to finish. This is why it gets read. |
| Next-issue promise | "See you in a couple of weeks" | Closes the loop, renews the subscription in the reader's head. |
| Explicit forward ask | "Forward it to colleagues so they can subscribe too" | The growth loop is stated, not hoped for. |

**Question-as-header is the piece that fits him best.** His whole method is
*how would we know?* — the format and the argument want the same thing.

## The Proof Over Promise issue template

```
PROOF OVER PROMISE
Evidence before claims · AI governance, model risk, professional judgment

[One-sentence thesis: what this issue is about and why it matters now.]

──────────────────────────────────────────

[Question as header — the one the reader also has]

  The thing that happened. A walkthrough, a document, a line in a
  framework. Concrete before abstract.

  The tension: what everyone assumes.

  The turn: what the evidence actually shows. One hard number,
  checkable.

  What it means for you on Monday.

[Second question as header]

  Same shape.

──────────────────────────────────────────

[Optional third item, only if it earns it. Two is the default.]

—Yasir

Next issue in two weeks. If something here is wrong, overstated, or too
confident, tell me: YasirAMalik@gmail.com — I would rather be corrected
than agreed with.

Written by Yasir A. Malik — bank examiner, fifteen years in internal
audit at Citigroup and JPMorgan Chase, DBA candidate at FIU.
Drafting assistance used and disclosed; the judgment is mine.

Forward this to someone who has to assure AI they did not build.
Subscribe: proofoverpromise.substack.com
```

**Length:** 1,200–1,800 words. Two items. Three only when the third genuinely
earns it — the discipline of *two* is what makes the Shortlist finishable.

**Every item needs its hard number.** If an item has no checkable figure, it is
an opinion with a header. Find the number or cut the item.

## LinkedIn — the public surface

Substack is canonical. LinkedIn is where it gets found. **Different shape, same
argument** — reposting the full issue performs badly and reads as a dump.

Post 2–4 days after the Substack issue, so the email list stays the reason to
subscribe:

```
[The question from the strongest item, as the opening line.]

[3-5 short paragraphs. The concrete thing that happened, the tension,
the turn, the number. One idea only — not both items.]

[The line that costs him something — the part where he was wrong, or
where the honest answer is unsettled. This is what gets replies.]

Full issue, including [what the other item covers]: [link]
```

Rules:
- **One item per post**, never the whole issue. The second item is a second
  post the following week if it stands alone.
- No hashtag walls. Three at most, and only ones a practitioner searches.
- The link goes at the end, after the argument has already paid off.
- **Never posted automatically.** He posts it, every time (safeguard 4).

## What gets counted

Recorded per issue in the Notion pipeline. Numbers from Substack's own
dashboard and LinkedIn's post analytics — never estimated, never rounded up.

| Metric | Why this one |
|---|---|
| Subscribers, total | The only number that compounds |
| Net new, this issue | Which topics actually recruit |
| Open rate | Whether the subject line earned the open |
| LinkedIn impressions / comments | Comments matter more — this audience argues |
| Replies to the correction invite | The real signal. A reader who tells him he is wrong is the reader the newsletter is for |

Track them from issue 01, when they are all zero. A baseline you did not
record is a baseline you do not have — which is the argument of the newsletter
applied to the newsletter.

## Monetizing — not yet, and the reason matters

Substack paid tiers, sponsorship and a paid community are all real options
later. None of them is the next decision.

The blocker is not strategy, it is inventory: **there is nothing to monetize
until issues exist on a kept schedule.** Six published issues on a fortnightly
cadence is the earliest point at which the question is worth asking, because
before that there is no evidence the cadence holds — and a newsletter called
Proof Over Promise cannot sell a subscription on a promise.

One thing to decide *before* then, because it is hard to reverse: whether the
advisory practice is ever pitched in the issue. Current position in `voice.md`
is no — the practice exists, the newsletter is not a funnel for it, and if an
issue reads as a pitch it has failed. Revisit deliberately, not by drift.
