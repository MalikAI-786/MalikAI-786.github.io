## Post

In 1915 a Punjabi poet writing about the structure of the self identified a failure mode that model risk teams are currently rediscovering, badly. He called it su'āl. Asking.

Muhammad Iqbal's argument in Asrār-i-Khudī is that a self is not a thing you have but an activity you sustain, and that it thins under specific conditions. Su'āl is the one he is harshest about. Anything received rather than earned weakens the receiver — not because the giver did wrong, but because a self that habituates to being supplied stops generating.

He meant it broadly. The borrowed opinion. The unearned position. The conclusion adopted because it arrived already formed.

I did not go looking for a hundred-year-old description of automation bias. It is simply the closest thing I have read to the mechanism my doctoral research is about, which is a narrow and uncomfortable question: when an AI system produces the analysis, does the reviewer still review it?

The evidence available so far suggests the problem is not that these systems are wrong. It is where they are agreeable. Systems tuned on human approval tend to affirm the position you already hold, which means they are least useful exactly where a reviewer most needs pushback. That is a control problem before it is a technology problem, and it does not get solved by a better model.

Iqbal's move, notably, is not to prohibit receiving. It is to treat the capacity to generate as something that decays with disuse and therefore has to be exercised deliberately. Read as a control principle rather than a moral one, that is unusually practical: the question is not whether your people use these tools, it is whether anything in the design still requires them to form a view of their own.

I am not sure what the right exercise is at an institutional scale. Requiring an independent view before the model's output is visible is the obvious candidate and it is expensive. I do not know yet what it costs or whether the cost is worth paying.

To be clear about what this is and is not: the argument above is mine and the evidence for it has to be mine. Iqbal described a structure. He did not write about model risk, and citing him proves nothing. If the point does not stand with the citation deleted, it does not stand.
