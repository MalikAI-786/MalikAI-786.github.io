## Post

A self-identified issue and an examiner-identified issue can describe exactly the same control failure. They are not the same finding, and every regulated institution knows it.

The difference is who looked first.

I spent years on both sides of that gap. At the OCC I was the one who looked. Inside two banks I was the one hoping we had looked already. The institutions that came out of examinations best were never the ones with the fewest problems. They were the ones who had found their own problems and could show the work.

So a while ago I turned the method inward and built a measuring instrument for myself. Not a habit tracker. A habit tracker records compliance, which is the easy question. I wanted the harder one an auditor actually asks: is this control designed correctly, and is it operating effectively?

The first design decision was the one that mattered, and it was uncomfortable.

There is no field where I record how I think I am doing. The headline rating is computed from the measures underneath it, each against a written rubric, and there is no way to move the number except by moving something real. I cannot award myself anything.

The second decision followed from it. The rating can go down. Standing is a computed position, not a ratchet, and absent sustained input it falls. A measure you can only accumulate is not a measure of a capacity. It is a record of having once qualified.

Both decisions make the instrument worse to use. That was the point. A scoring field you can reach directly will be reached directly, and it will be reached hardest on exactly the days when the honest score would be bad.

Which puts a question to most of what passes for self-assessment in a control function. If your control self-assessment has a box where the owner states the residual rating, you are not measuring the control. You are measuring the owner's appetite for a difficult conversation.

I do not yet know whether an instrument this unforgiving survives contact with a bad month, or whether the honest failure mode is that you simply stop opening it. That is a real question about control design and I cannot answer it yet.

The design is written up in full — the five decisions, what each one costs, and where the whole thing is weak. Link in the first comment.

## Notes

**What I deliberately left out.** The Qur'anic and Iqbalian frame is absent from
this post, and that is a choice rather than an omission. Post 001 has to
establish that he is entitled to the argument before it introduces the
vocabulary. The tradition arrives in 003 (Su'āl), by which point the reader has
seen him reason as a practitioner first. Introducing *muḥāsaba* here would have
made the launch post read as the thing the whole register rule exists to
prevent.

The series title still holds — this *is* M1, and the concept of muḥāsaba is the
post's spine. It simply runs untranslated.

**The `[UNVERIFIED]` that was avoided rather than resolved.** An earlier shape
of this post opened on *Ḥāsibū anfusakum qabla an tuḥāsabū* — "take account of
yourselves before you are taken account of" — which is the instrument's own
epigraph. It is dropped here because the attribution needs checking first. See
Gaps.

**On the OCC/bank sentence.** "I was the one who looked / I was the one hoping
we had looked already" is a characterisation of two roles, both of which are on
his public profile. It asserts no specific examination, institution, finding or
outcome. Keep it that way.

**Optional image.** If one is used, it should be a typographic card carrying the
line "A measure you can only accumulate is not a measure of a capacity." Built
from the brand system. No screenshot of the instrument, ever — Safeguard 3.

**Reply to expect.** Someone will ask to see the instrument. The answer is in
the case study and should be given warmly and without apology: the data is the
reason it works, and the data is why you cannot see it.

## Gaps

- `[UNVERIFIED: attribution]` — *Ḥāsibū anfusakum qabla an tuḥāsabū* is
  conventionally attributed to ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and is **not** a Qur'anic
  verse, though it is widely quoted as though it were. It is not used in this
  draft for that reason. Before it appears in any post, he needs to settle the
  attribution and the chain he is willing to stand behind in public. The
  instrument's own epigraph should be checked at the same time.
- "A while ago" is deliberately vague because the build date is not in the
  source material. If he wants a date, he supplies it. Do not estimate one.
- No metric appears in this post. None was available that would not have been
  an assertion about himself, and inventing one here would have been the exact
  failure the post is about.
