Identity System · v1

The Reference Mark.

An identity built from a measuring instrument rather than a logo trend. An open ring, an anchor node, and a reference line extended past the figure. Ember marks what pulls you. Verdigris marks what was verified. Everything on this page is driven by one token file — if a swatch here is wrong, the token is wrong.

The Mark

An instrument, not an emblem.

Three elements, each carrying an argument the practice actually makes.

The open ring

A recurring engagement, deliberately not closed. A finished loop would say the judgment is settled. It isn't — that is the whole subject.

The node

The anchor, floating in the break rather than sitting on the ring. The anchor is the thing pulling on the judgment, and it is not part of the process.

The extended crossbar

A reference line pushed past the letterform, reaching for the ring without closing on it. Measurement that keeps going after the figure stops.

Micro cut · below 32px

Two cuts, one mark

The primary cut's interior clearances are 2.3–3.8px in a 64px box. Below roughly 32px those close up and the mark turns to mud. The micro cut opens the break, fattens both strokes and drops the overshoot. Use it in favicons, avatars and nav bars.

Clear space

One ring radius on every side — half the mark's own height. Nothing sets inside it: no type, no rule, no image edge.

Misuse

Don't

Stretch or condense. The ring is a circle; distortion is immediately legible.

Don't

Rotate. The break sits upper-left and the crossbar reads level. Both are load-bearing.

Don't

Recolour the ring. Ember or a single flat ink — never another hue, never a gradient.

Don't

Set ember on ember. On an ember ground the mark goes to warm black, full stop.

Colour

Ember, ink, and one quiet green.

Ember was sampled from the leather of a physical badge under tungsten light, white-balanced against the card behind it, and cleaned to #E0662E. The rest of the system is built to serve it.

Ember

What pulls you. The anchor, the signal, the thing demanding attention. Spend it sparingly — one ember element per view carries the weight.

Ink

The record. Warm graphite on warm paper, never pure black on cold grey. A cold neutral beside this ember reads as a mistake.

Verdigris

What was verified. Deliberately quiet — evidence doesn't shout. Never swapped with ember for visual variety; the semantics are the point.

The rule that shapes everything

Ember 500 is 3.11:1 on paper. It can carry rules, fills, marks and large display type — and it can never carry body text on a light ground. That single constraint is why the ramp has a 650 step, why the eyebrows and small labels sit in #AD4317, and why buttons put warm-black on ember rather than white. Designing around it is what keeps the palette from going soft.

Verified pairs

Every combination the system actually ships, measured against WCAG 2.1. Body text is held to 4.5:1; marks, rules and large display type to 3:1.

Three voices.

Not three fonts chosen for variety — three jobs. Each voice says a different kind of thing, and mixing them up is the fastest way to make the work look like marketing.

Working Rules

Seven things that keep it honest.

  1. 01Ember never carries body text on a light ground. Ember 650 does.
  2. 02One ember element per view carries the weight. If two things shout, neither is a signal.
  3. 03Ember marks what pulls you; verdigris marks what was verified. Never swap them for visual variety.
  4. 04Warm neutrals only. A cold grey next to this ember reads as an error.
  5. 05Below 32px, use the micro cut. The primary cut's clearances close up and the mark turns to mud.
  6. 06Clear space is one ring radius. Nothing sets inside it.
  7. 07The mark is ember, warm black, or reversed white — never a third hue, never a gradient.

Provenance

On where this came from: the ember was sampled from a photograph of an existing badge, and the idea of a letterform inside a broken ring is a shared one. Everything drawn here is original geometry — a different break angle, a floating anchor node, an overshooting crossbar, and a two-cut size system — built to carry this practice's own argument. It is a family resemblance, not a copy, and it should never be presented as any other organisation's mark.
