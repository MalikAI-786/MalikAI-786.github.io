# Prompt · Decompose a qualitative article

Self-contained. Paste the whole file, then the article text.

```
INPUTS
  ARTICLE:  <full text, or a link if the model can retrieve it>

OUTPUTS
  Seven headed sections. No preamble, no summary of the topic.
```

---

Decompose the article against this frame. The purpose is **not** to learn the
article's topic — it is to see how the qualitative work was done.

**1. Research problem** — what gap or difficulty prompted the study, and why the
authors argue it matters. Both halves; a problem without a stated importance is
half a problem.

**2. Research question** — quote it if stated. If it is not stated explicitly,
say so, then reconstruct it and mark the reconstruction clearly. Many
qualitative papers bury the question; that is a weakness worth noticing.

**3. Context** — who, where, when, what setting. This is what makes the findings
context-dependent rather than general.

**4. Systematic approach** — which of the five (narrative, phenomenology,
grounded theory, ethnography, case study), and what in the paper shows it. If
approaches are combined, say which governs sampling and which governs analysis.

**5. Data collection** — every source, with counts and durations where given.
Name the triangulation explicitly if there is any. Say whether the design is
longitudinal.

**6. Data analysis** — inductive or deductive. First-order and second-order, if
present. Note whether collection and analysis ran in parallel.

**7. Complex account** — how the findings are reported. What is the final
structure: phases, a process model, a set of propositions?

Then, in two lines: **what the researcher's role was**, and **what the paper
does about bias**.

Quote the article for anything contested. Where the paper is silent on one of
the seven, write "not stated" — do not fill the gap.
