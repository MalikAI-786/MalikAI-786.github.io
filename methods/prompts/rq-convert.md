# Prompt · Convert a quantitative RQ to qualitative

Self-contained. Paste the whole file into any chat window.

```
INPUTS
  QUANT_RQ:     <the existing quantitative research question>
  PHENOMENON:   <the thing being experienced — usually the dependent variable, restated as a process>
  WHO:          <the population, specifically>
  WHERE:        <the context — sector, org type, setting>

OUTPUTS
  Three candidate questions, then a rule check on each. Nothing else.
```

---

Convert QUANT_RQ into three candidate qualitative research questions.

**Every candidate must satisfy all six rules:**

1. Opens with **how** or **what**. Never *why* — it invites justification rather
   than description.
2. **Open-ended.** No answer embedded in the question.
3. **Non-directional.** Does not presume the effect exists, or that it is
   positive or negative.
4. Contains **none** of: *factors, variables, impact, effect, relationship,
   influence, determinants, predictors, correlation, significant*. These carry
   measurement assumptions the qualitative phase has not earned.
5. Names **who** and carries **context**.
6. Ends with a **question mark**.

Vary the three by framing: one on *described experience*, one on *process or
navigation*, one on *lived experience of the phenomenon*.

**Then check each candidate against all six rules in a table**, marking pass or
fail per rule and quoting the offending word on any failure.

Note on rule 4: models converting quantitative questions insert *factors*
almost every time. Check your own output for it before returning.

A note on directionality, since it is the rule most often missed: if the
construct itself names a distortion — *bias*, *over-reliance*, *failure* —
the question is already directional. Reframe to the underlying **process**
and let the analysis find the distortion, or not.
