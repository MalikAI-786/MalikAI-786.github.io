# Prompt · Extract propositions from codes

> ⚠️ Operates on coded output derived from participant data. Same handling rules
> as `codebook-build.md`.

Self-contained. Paste the whole file, then the coding output.

```
INPUTS
  SECOND_ORDER:  <the second-order themes and their first-order codes>
  RQ:            <the research question>
  CONSTRUCTS:    <existing quantitative constructs, if the propositions must map back>

OUTPUTS
  Numbered propositions, a mapping table, and a list of what cannot be proposed.
```

---

Convert the themes into **propositions** — statements that could be tested but
have not been.

**Rules:**

- Phrase as a **proposition**, not a hypothesis. No directional prediction, no
  significance language, no null form. "P1: Auditors who X tend to Y" — not "H1:
  X is positively associated with Y."
- Each proposition traces to **named second-order themes**, which trace to
  first-order codes, which trace to quotes. Show the chain. A proposition that
  cannot be traced is an opinion.
- Say what would **disconfirm** it. A proposition no observation could
  contradict is not a proposition.

**1. Propositions:** numbered, each with its trace and its disconfirming case.

**2. Mapping table**, if CONSTRUCTS is supplied:

| Proposition | Maps to construct | New construct needed? | Candidate item wording |

**3. Cannot be proposed** — themes that are real in the data but do not support
a testable statement. Say why. Description that resists operationalisation is a
legitimate finding, and forcing it into a proposition is how qualitative work
gets misused.

Do not inflate. Three defensible propositions beat nine that stretch the data.
