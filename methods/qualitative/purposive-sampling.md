# Purposive sampling

## What it is
Selecting participants deliberately, against stated criteria, because of what
they can tell you. Not random, and not aiming at representativeness.

## When it applies
Whenever the goal is depth in a context rather than inference to a population.
Criteria are set in advance and written down, so that selection can be audited
afterwards. Sample to **saturation** — the point at which new data stops
producing new categories — and record when it was reached rather than deciding
a number up front.

## What it cannot do
It cannot support a population claim, and calling it a "sample" invites readers
to expect one. It gives no protection against the researcher recruiting people
who will confirm what they already think, which is why the criteria have to be
written before recruiting starts. Saturation is also a judgement, not a
measurement, and claiming it without the coding record to show is unfalsifiable.

## Citation
Patton; Creswell & Poth.

## How I explain this
"Random sampling protects you from choosing wrong. Purposive sampling means you
chose on purpose — so the reasoning has to be written down where someone can
check it."
