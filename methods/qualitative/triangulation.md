# Triangulation

## What it is
Using more than one source of evidence on the same question, so that a claim
does not rest on any single account.

## When it applies
Always, in practice. The useful version is not "three methods" but **three
sources chosen because each catches what the others miss** — an interview gives
the account, an observation gives the behaviour, a document gives the record.

## What it cannot do
It does not make a finding true. Three sources sharing one bias agree with each
other and are all wrong together — an interview with a chief executive, an
observation of a meeting they chair, and minutes their office wrote are one
source in three formats. It also does not resolve disagreement between sources.
When they disagree, that *is* the finding, and flattening it into an average
destroys the most interesting thing in the dataset.

## Citation
Denzin; Lincoln & Guba (1985); Creswell & Poth.

## How I explain this
"If your three sources all come from the same mouth, you have one source."
