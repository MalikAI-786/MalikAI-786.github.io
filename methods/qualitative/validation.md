# Validating a qualitative study

## What it is
The set of practices that let a reader judge whether an interpretation can be
trusted. The quantitative vocabulary of validity and reliability does not
transfer; the parallel criteria are credibility, transferability, dependability
and confirmability.

## When it applies
From design onward, not at write-up. Each technique defends against a specific
failure:

| Technique | Defends against |
| --- | --- |
| Member checking | The reading drifting from what was meant |
| Insider / outsider pairing | Immersion becoming identification |
| Audit trail | Reasoning reconstructed after the conclusion |
| Reflexivity journal | Unexamined position shaping what is heard |
| Triangulation | A single source taken at face value |
| Thick description | A reader being unable to judge transferability |

## What it cannot do
None of it makes findings generalisable — transferability is a judgement the
*reader* makes about their own context, and the author's job is only to supply
enough description for that judgement to be possible. Member checking has its
own failure mode: participants may correct an accurate reading because it is
unflattering, and an account revised to keep participants comfortable is worse
evidence, not better.

## Citation
Lincoln & Guba (1985); Creswell & Poth.

## How I explain this
"Can I replicate it? Only in the same context. Can I trust it? Only if you show
me the trail you left while you were deciding."
