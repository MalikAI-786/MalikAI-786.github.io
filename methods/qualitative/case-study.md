# Case study

## What it is
Deep examination of a bounded system — an organisation, a programme, a process,
a decision — using multiple sources of evidence. Single or multiple case.

## When it applies
When the phenomenon cannot be separated from its context, and when the boundary
is genuinely definable. **Multiple cases are where it earns its keep**, because
comparison across cases is the analytic move a single case cannot make. Two or
three cases going through the same transition is a substantially stronger design
than one studied in more depth.

## What it cannot do
It cannot generalise to a population. A single case cannot compare. And the
boundary is a claim, not a given — a case whose edges are drawn to fit the data
is not bounded, it is selected.

## Citation
Yin; Creswell & Poth.

## How I explain this
"One case tells you what happened there. Three cases start telling you what
travels."
