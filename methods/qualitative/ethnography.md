# Ethnography

## What it is
The researcher embedded in the setting over an extended period, studying a
culture-sharing group from inside it. Longitudinal by nature — observation,
interviews and documents accumulated over months or years.

## When it applies
When the object is culture or a process as it actually unfolds, and when the
account has to be built from being there rather than from being told about it
afterwards. Pairing an embedded insider with a deliberately distant outsider is
the standard defence: the insider gets access, the outsider holds interpretive
distance.

## What it cannot do
It cannot be done quickly or at low cost — the time commitment is the method.
Access at this depth is rarely granted, and the resulting work is often not
publishable because it belongs to the organisation. Embeddedness creates a real
conflict-of-interest exposure, and the presence of a known researcher changes
the behaviour being recorded.

## Citation
Van Maanen (1979); Creswell & Poth.

## How I explain this
"You don't interview the culture. You live in it long enough to stop noticing
it, and then you write down what you stopped noticing."
