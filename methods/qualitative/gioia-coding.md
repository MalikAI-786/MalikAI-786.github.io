# First-order and second-order analysis

## What it is
Two analytic levels reported separately. **First-order** stays in the
participant's own words and categories. **Second-order** is the researcher's
theoretical reading of those categories. Structured together, they show the
route from informant terms to theoretical claim.

## When it applies
Whenever a reader needs to see where the informant stops and the author begins.
It is the single most effective device for making an inductive claim auditable,
and it is what allows a reader to disagree with the interpretation while still
accepting the data.

## What it cannot do
It does not confer rigour by formatting. A structure filled in after the
conclusion was reached is presentation, not analysis. The separation is also
harder than it looks: a first-order code phrased in the researcher's vocabulary
has already crossed the line, and once crossed it cannot be uncrossed by
relabelling.

## Citation
Van Maanen (1979); Gioia & Chittipeddi (1991); Gioia, Corley & Hamilton (2013).

## How I explain this
"Two columns. What they said, and what I think it means. Never one column."
