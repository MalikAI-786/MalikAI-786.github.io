# Constant comparison

## What it is
Every new piece of data is compared against what has already been coded, rather
than coded on its own. Categories are continuously revised as they meet data
that does not fit.

## When it applies
Wherever collection and analysis run in parallel, which in qualitative work is
the norm rather than an optimisation. After a handful of interviews there is
enough to compare against; from that point every subsequent interview is both
new data and a test of the existing scheme.

## What it cannot do
It cannot be retrofitted. Comparison performed after all collection is finished
is just coding, and the record will show it. It also tends to stabilise
categories early, which is efficient but can close off a reading that a later
transcript would have opened — the defence is to log category changes, including
the ones rejected, as they happen.

## Citation
Glaser & Strauss (1967); Charmaz (2006).

## How I explain this
"You are not filing transcripts into folders. Each one asks whether the folders
were right."
