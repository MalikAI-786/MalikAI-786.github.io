# Grounded theory

## What it is
An inductive method that builds theory upward from data rather than testing
theory downward against it. Coding, comparison and sampling continue in
parallel until categories stop changing. It ends in propositions.

## When it applies
When little is established and a testable structure is the goal. It is the most
rigorous of the five approaches on data analysis, and it is the one that bridges
most cleanly back to quantitative work, because propositions convert into items.
It can also be used purely as an analytic technique inside another approach —
running grounded-theory coding over case study or phenomenological data without
adopting the whole methodology.

## What it cannot do
It cannot be done casually. The procedural demands — theoretical sampling,
saturation, memoing throughout — are heavy, and a study that skips them is not
grounded theory regardless of what it is called. The purist position that the
researcher should approach the data with no prior literature is not workable for
doctoral research and should not be claimed. And propositions are not findings:
they are statements awaiting a test.

## Citation
Glaser & Strauss (1967); Strauss & Corbin; Charmaz (2006).

## How I explain this
"Everything else describes. This one ends with something you could go and test.
That is why it costs more to do."
